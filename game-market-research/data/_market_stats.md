## 2. Revenue per game: median, mean and distribution (Steam)

### 2a. The "$249 median / 0.5% over $1M" lead: partly verified, with a conflict
- **Median gross revenue for a 2025 Steam release: $249** (≈ $174 after Valve's 30%) [ESTIMATE – Ziva blog, citing VG Insights + Alinea Analytics; 2025 calendar-year revenue].
  The figure appears in Ziva and also in Shane the Gamer, but Shane the Gamer frames it as the median *Steam game*, not specifically indie, and probably takes it from the same underlying chart. So it is **one data lineage, not two independent sources.**
- Ziva/VG Insights/Alinea tier table for 2025 releases (n ≈ 19,000) [ESTIMATE]:
  - < $100: ~30% (~5,700 games)
  - $100–$1k: ~36% (~6,800)
  - $1k–$50k: ~24% (~4,600)
  - $50k–$1M: ~9.5% (~1,800)
  - **> $1M: ~0.5% (~95 games)**
  - So **66% earned < $1k** and **90% earned < $50k**.
- **Conflict.** Alinea Analytics' own year-end posts say **~300 of ~20,000 2025 releases grossed > $1M (≈ 1.5%)** [ESTIMATE – Alinea, Dec 2025]. That is three times the 0.5% in Ziva's chart.
  A likely explanation is that the Ziva chart counts 2025 calendar-year revenue only, or uses a different cut-off date. **For the report, use "0.5–1.5% of 2025 releases grossed over $1M"**, or cite Alinea's ~300 as the more primary figure.

### 2b. Independent corroboration from Gamalytic (2025 releases, Oct 2025 snapshot)
- **65.9% (8,388 games) earned < $1,000** [ESTIMATE – Gamalytic, via MP1st / DualShockers / GamesRadar, Oct 2025]. This independently matches the "66% < $1k" figure above.
- **~40% earned < $100**, i.e. less than the Steam Direct fee. The lowest 30% averaged **$37** [ESTIMATE – Gamalytic, Oct 2025].
- **~8% grossed > $100k** [ESTIMATE – Gamalytic aggregates, quoted by analyst Smiarowski, Oct 2025].
- Unit bands for 2025 releases [ESTIMATE – Gamalytic, Oct 2025]: < 100 copies 47.5%; 100–1k 28%; 1k–10k 16%; 10k–50k 5.4%; 50k–100k 1.2%; > 100k 1.8%.

### 2c. Valve's official figure
- **5,863 titles earned > $100,000 on Steam in 2025**, up from ~3,000 in 2020 [CONFIRMED – Valve, Tom Giardino, GDC 2026 Steam Business talk]. This counts *all* titles on Steam, not only 2025 releases.
  Valve said more titles than in earlier years also pass $500k and $1M, but gave no counts.
- About **1,700 of the 5,863 were 2025 launches** [ESTIMATE – Simon Carless, via Ziva]. 1,700 ÷ ~20,300 ≈ **8.4%**, consistent with Gamalytic's 8%.

### 2d. Shares under $5k and $10k
- No source publishes a direct revenue share for < $5k or < $10k for 2025.
- **Derived [ESTIMATE, this report]:** 75.5% of 2025 releases sold < 1,000 copies (Gamalytic). At a typical realised price of $5–$15, that is under ~$5k–$15k gross.
  Combined with "66% < $1k" and "90% < $50k", a reasonable bracket is **~75–80% of 2025 releases grossed < $10k** and ~70–75% grossed < $5k. Treat these as interpolations, not measurements.

### 2e. Mean versus median (skew)
- **Average debut game: ~$120k gross; third game: ~$209k** [ESTIMATE – VG Insights, via VoxBooster and Ziva]. Against a median of $249, this shows that a handful of hits drive the mean.
- Older medians for context: lifetime median $3,947 for indie Steam games (VG Insights 2020, via Ziva); lifetime median $1,136 for the 2019 cohort (Steam Page Analyzer).
  Steam Page Analyzer also gives "$5k–$15k lifetime median for indies with commercial intent". It publishes no sample size, so it is low reliability.
- No clean *mean per 2025 release* is available. Do not divide the $4.4B indie revenue by the number of 2025 releases: that revenue includes the back catalogue.

### 2f. Long tail after launch
- **Median Year-1 revenue = 2.46× Week-1 revenue** (2023–25 releases), about 20% lower than in 2021. Games priced ≥ $10 have a slightly better tail [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo].
  An earlier 2024 survey of 100+ games gave 3.16× (2.69× for games with ≥ 1k Week-1 copies).
- **Early Access graduates (2025):** median 30-day revenue at 1.0 ≈ 40% of median 30-day Early Access launch revenue [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo].

## 3. Games reaching 1,000+ reviews (2025) – lead verified
- **608 of 20,282 games released in 2025 (2.99%) reached ≥ 1,000 Steam reviews** [ESTIMATE – Chris Zukowski / How To Market A Game, VG Insights data sampled 2026-01-04; reported by GamesRadar and Softonic].
- The trend is up: 2.44% in 2024, 2.56% in 2023 and 2.74% in 2022 [same source].
- Zukowski notes that 1,000 is a conservative bar. Many games earn $150k+ and get Daily Deals with ~500 reviews.
- **About half of 2025 releases have fewer than 10 reviews** [ESTIMATE – SteamDB via GamingOnLinux / MP1st, Dec 2025]. Another count found 50.3% of 20,636 2025 games had reached 10 reviews [ESTIMATE – secondary analysis, 2026].
- Valve Daily Deals in 2025: 1,500+ games featured, 69% for the first time; 8.2M customers bought deals (+125% YoY) [CONFIRMED – Valve GDC 2026].

## 4. Indie share of Steam revenue – lead verified
- **Indie ≈ 25% of Steam gross revenue in 2025: $4.4–4.5B of ~$17.7B** [ESTIMATE – Alinea Analytics]. $17.7B is a running total for 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-19, so the full year is slightly higher. Steam was up ~15% from $15.4B in 2024.
- **Top 5 new 2025 indies (Schedule I, R.E.P.O., PEAK, Silksong, Escape from Duckov) grossed > $500M combined, ≈ 3% of Steam's 2025 total** [ESTIMATE – Alinea].
- Steam's December 2025 gross was $1.6B, the highest month on record at that time [ESTIMATE – Alinea via Notebookcheck].
- Different definition: VG Insights put "indie" at **48% of Steam revenue and 58% of units** (period up to Sep 2024, broad tag-based definition) [ESTIMATE – VG Insights]. This uses a different definition and is not a contradiction.
- 2026 context: Steam H1 2026 gross **$11.1B (record half-year, +14.5%)**; first 9 months **$16.5B vs $14.5B** in the same period of 2025; **September 2026 $1.7B** (record September, +13%) [ESTIMATE – Alinea].
  Among the top-500 grossing games in Sep 2026, **new 2026 IP took only 20.5% of revenue**, against 79.5% for established IP [ESTIMATE – Alinea via Tom's Hardware]. Back-catalogue dominance is a risk for newcomers.

## 5. Review-to-sales ratio (Boxleiter / NB number)
- **GameDiscoverCo "NB number" (New Boxleiter), 2020:** 237 non-outlier games; **mean 63 sales per review, median 58**; middle 80% between 25× and 100× [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo developer-submitted data].
  - By launch year: pre-2017 mean 81 / median 74; 2019 mean 57 / median 51; **2020 mean 41 / median 38**.
  - The drop followed Valve's late-October 2019 change that prompts players to review after a few hours of play.
  - Several 2025–26 blogs re-quote "63" as a 2025 figure. That is a mis-citation of the 2020 dataset.
- **GameDiscoverCo 2022 update:** the multiplier for *hot new hit games* rose from ~30× to almost **60×**. Hits draw casual buyers who review less [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo via GameWorldObserver, 2022-11-15].
  A secondary summary of a later GameDiscoverCo cut gives **52.8× for games with 1k–10k reviews vs ~36× for games with < 100 reviews**. This is secondary only, so verify it before citing.
- **Gamalytic deep dive (~2023):** **93 copies per review in 2013 → 32 in 2023**.
  - Games with an average discount ≥ 30% run **40–57×**.
  - Asian players leave reviews slightly more often, but the demographic correlation was **not statistically significant**. The ratio "varies a lot between games and is hard to predict" [ESTIMATE – Gamalytic].
- **VG Insights (2021, >10,000 games, calibrated on leaked 2018 data for 11,445 games):** >90% correlation between reviews and units. **2020+ releases mostly 20–55×**; pre-2014 releases 40–100× [ESTIMATE – VG Insights].
- **Price:** Steam Page Analyzer (2026) says VG Insights and Gamalytic both found that *higher-priced games have lower multiples*, and that Gamalytic measured **free games at ~2× the sales per review of paid games**.
  Its price table (< $5: 35–55×; $40+: 15–25×) has no stated source, so it is low reliability.
- **Genre:** a calculator blog lists Action/FPS 50–80× and Indie/Narrative 20–40× (Sports 55×, RPG 50×, Adventure/Sim 40×). This is unsourced and low reliability.
- **China share:** no study was found that measures sales per review for China-heavy games.
  - Chinese-language reviews skew more negative.
  - Valve now shows language-specific review scores for games with ≥ 2,000 reviews and ≥ 200 in a language [CONFIRMED – Valve change, via Kotaku].
  - The spec's 50–60× for China-heavy games is a *working assumption*, not an evidenced figure.
- **Friendslop calibration points (no review counts gathered, so ratios not computed):**
  - R.E.P.O.: ~19M downloads, > $152M revenue [ESTIMATE – AppMagic].
  - Schedule I: > 10.2M copies, > $189M lifetime revenue [ESTIMATE – AppMagic].
- VG Insights now redirects to Sensor Tower [ESTIMATE – Steam Page Analyzer, 2026], so its free data may no longer be available.

## 6. Wishlist-to-sales conversion
- **Median first-week sales ÷ launch wishlists = 0.15× for games launching with > 25k wishlists** (past-year cohort, 2024–25). This includes organic, non-wishlist sales [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo]. **Lead verified.**
- **For games priced > $10, the median drops to 0.10×** [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo].
- Conversion varies by an **order of magnitude** between projects. There is no big trend apart from a December seasonal dip [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo].
- Older baseline: in a 2023 cohort of ~700 games with ≥ 5k wishlists, median first-week conversion was **~22% (0.22×)** [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo, earlier study as summarised in search results]. The newer 0.10–0.15× shows conversion has weakened.
- Different metric: "only 10% of new Steam games have a wishlist-to-sales conversion rate higher than 1.9%" [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo benchmark via GameWorldObserver, 2024-12-06]. This appears to be a per-period conversion of outstanding wishlists, not first-week sales ÷ wishlists. Do not mix it with the 0.15× figure.
- **Practical rule for the report:** first-week units ≈ 0.10–0.20 × launch wishlists, with ~0.15× as the central case for well-wishlisted, cheap games. Year-1 revenue ≈ 2.5× Week-1 revenue (see 2f).

## 7. The "friendslop" trend
**Definition.** "Friendslop" is informal and initially pejorative slang. Per Wikipedia it was coined in a March 2025 social-media post.
- It means cheap (typically $5–$20), small-team, online co-op games built for groups of friends: proximity voice chat, clumsy physics comedy, simple goals, emergent "clip-able" moments.
- The template is *Lethal Company* (2023); precursors include Phasmophobia (2020) and Left 4 Dead (2008).
- After PEAK (June 2025) the word became largely descriptive, though it is still used to call copycats cash-ins.

**2025–2026 hits**
- **4 co-op titles were in Steam's top-10 premium games by copies sold in 2025:** R.E.P.O., PEAK, Schedule I and RV There Yet? [ESTIMATE – AppMagic].
- Price band: most are $5–$20. One buyer typically pulls in ~3 friends, so each "sale" is ~4 copies [ESTIMATE / analysis – AppMagic].
- R.E.P.O.: ~19M downloads, > $152M revenue. Schedule I: > 10.2M copies, > $189M (base price $19.99) [ESTIMATE – AppMagic].
- **Mage Arena:** ~624,000 copies and ~$3.69M in its debut week, with only 11.75% of friendslop players at its peak [ESTIMATE – Chris Zukowski / HTMAG]. A mid-tier share can still be very profitable.
- **Genre-wide concurrent players:**
  - Record ~281k after R.E.P.O. (March 2025).
  - Then **~350k (HTMAG) / ~366k (GameWorldObserver)** when **Meccha Chameleon** launched in June 2026 [ESTIMATE – SteamDB-based charts, HTMAG 2026-07-30 / GWO 2026-08-03].

**Saturation or not**
- Zukowski's view (Jul 2026): **not saturated**. Demand keeps growing, and the audience is "content-hungry", switching games every few months.
  - Pattern: **a top launch captures > 50% of genre concurrent players, then fades to ~10% within 3–9 months.**
  - He argues release counts alone don't show saturation. In 2026, 67 battle royale, 785 RPG and 1,087 incremental games were released, and each genre still had ~$4M earners [ESTIMATE – HTMAG].
- Counter-view: critics (AV Club, The Daily Fandom) describe a flood of rushed cash-in copycats and "oversaturation". This is opinion; no quantitative release count for friendslop was found.
- **Risk read:** demand is resilient, but hits are short-lived. Each new title must be different enough to take the genre's attention for a few months, and most imitators are not.

**Role of streamers and TikTok**
- R.E.P.O. entered **Twitch's top-10 games within a week** of its 2025-02-26 Early Access launch.
  - Its Twitch viewership spiked after big creators (Valkyrae, ironmouse, Sykkuno, auronplay) played it together.
  - **All-time Twitch peak was 163.8k viewers (2025-04-12).** Viewership appeared to come from Fall Guys' audience [ESTIMATE – Streams Charts].
- Streams Charts' thesis: games that let streamers **team up** go viral on livestreams, and graphics and story become secondary.
- TikTok: the #friendslop tag and promo accounts (e.g., "Friendslop Gaming") are active, and devs post "when players call your game friendslop" clips. **No hard data** links TikTok views to sales. Evidence is anecdotal.
- AppMagic attributes sales to price and group buying, not creators. No source quantifies how much of sales comes from streamers.

## 8. Steam Next Fest and breakouts

## 11. Browser portals (Poki, CrazyGames and others): earnings per 1,000 plays
**No portal publishes a revenue-per-1,000-plays figure.** CrazyGames' own docs name "revenue per 1,000 plays" as the key developer metric but give no benchmark. What exists:

- **Revenue shares:**
  - Poki: **100% on traffic you bring, 50% on Poki-sourced traffic** [CONFIRMED – Poki developer portal, via Cinevva].
  - CrazyGames: **60% ads / 70% IAP** (2026 jam terms; not stated in its main docs) [ESTIMATE – Cinevva].
  - Others: GameMonetize 45%; Playgama Bridge 80%; GameDistribution 33% of net; Y8 50%; itch.io 90% default [ESTIMATE – Cinevva comparison table].
- **Ad rates (gross, per 1,000 ad views, not per play):** rewarded video ~**$15–28 eCPM in the US, $8–15 in the EU, $1–3 in tier-3 markets**. Banners pay a small fraction of that [ESTIMATE – Playgama 2026 breakdown via Cinevva].
- **The only hard per-play data point is old:** one developer earned **€556.92 from 451,327 CrazyGames plays across 8 WebGL games, ≈ €1.20 per 1,000 plays** (games launched ~2018) [ESTIMATE – developer-reported, via Cinevva]. It is not a 2026 benchmark.
- **Income ranges:**
  - A well-performing casual game on a major portal earns **~$200–$2,000/month** [ESTIMATE – Cinevva guides]. Another guide gives ~$500–$3,000/month for a first web game.
  - Poki says its **top developers earn $50k–$1M per year**, with top-developer revenue up ~10× in five years [CONFIRMED – Poki press release, Dec 2025; the lower bound has a typo in the original].
- **Scale:**
  - Poki had **625M players in 2025**, ~100M monthly players, **1B gameplays in a single month**, 1,500+ curated titles and 600+ developers [CONFIRMED – Poki].
  - CrazyGames reports **250M+ gameplays/month** [CONFIRMED – CrazyGames press].
  - Example: Emolingo Games (2 founders, now 5 people): 8 Poki games, each > 10M plays; Rainbow Obby has 100M plays; ~800k plays/day across its titles. Revenue was not disclosed [CONFIRMED – Poki blog, 2025-07-17].
- **Derived [ESTIMATE, this report]: developer revenue ≈ $0.5–$4 per 1,000 plays, ~$1–2 as a central case.**
  - Method: 1–2 ad impressions per play, at a blended global eCPM of ~$2–8, after a 50–60% portal share.
  - Cross-check: a studio at Emolingo's ~800k plays/day (~290M/yr) would gross ~$0.3–1.2M/yr at $1–4 per 1,000 plays. That is consistent with Poki's "top developers $50k–$1M" range.
  - Implication: **about 1M plays ≈ $500–$4,000** for the developer. Web portals make money at volume, not per game.

## 12. AI use in indie games and player reaction
**Steam AI-disclosure policy**
- Valve introduced a mandatory generative-AI disclosure on Steam in **January 2024** [CONFIRMED – Valve; see the Haro census and PC Gamer coverage].
- **The form was revised in January 2026.** Developers now disclose AI only if it "ships with your game, and is consumed by players", including store-page, community and marketing assets [CONFIRMED – Valve form change, via Game Developer / PCGamesN / Notebookcheck / Dexerto].
  - It asks separately about **pre-rendered** AI content and **live-generated** AI content or code during gameplay.
  - Behind-the-scenes tools (coding assistants like Copilot, bug checkers, office tools) are now **exempt**.
  - Developers must still guard against illegal or infringing output; uncontrolled live-generated AI can get a game removed.

**Share of games that disclose AI**
- **Totally Human (Ichiro Lambe), 2025-07-13** [ESTIMATE – Steam API analysis, via VGC / Insider Gaming / WN Hub]:
  - **7,818 of ~114,126 Steam games (~7%)** disclose AI, up from 1.1% in Apr 2024.
  - **~1 in 5 (≈ 20%) of 2025 releases** disclose AI.
  - ~60% of disclosed uses are visual assets.
  - Disclosure counts are a floor: some developers don't disclose, and others word disclosures defensively.
- **Sulka Haro census (~53,600 releases, mid-2023 to mid-2026)** [ESTIMATE – via PC Gamer, the fragwyz Substack and Cinevva]:
  - **10.9% of 2024 releases, 19.9% of 2025 releases, 30.8% of 2026 releases (YTD, July 2026)** carry an AI disclosure.
  - That is ~530 AI-flagged launches per month vs ~1,320 non-AI.
  - On trend, the share would pass **50% of releases in 2027–28**.
  - AI-disclosed games are ~⅓ of releases but only **~10–27% of estimated sales**.
  - ~22% of significant-revenue AI games added the flag *after* launch.
  - A later snapshot put the share at ~33%.

**Player attitudes (surveys)**
- **GameDiscoverCo survey of ~3,800 engaged Steam users** [ESTIMATE – GameDiscoverCo, via Gamereactor / WN Hub / games.gg]:
  - only **8.1%** won't consider any game that uses AI under any circumstances
  - **23.4%** have "absolutely no problem" with it
  - 19.6% are "fine" with it
- **Circana PlayerPulse, Dec 2025 (US):** just over **25%** say knowing a game used genAI for art, dialogue, text or music makes them less likely to buy it, or unwilling to buy it (up from ~22% in Mar 2024). **< 10%** say it makes them more interested [ESTIMATE – Circana, via Outlook Respawn].
- **Quantic Foundry (N = 1,799, Oct–Dec 2025, self-selected):** **85%** negative about genAI in games; 63% chose the most negative option [ESTIMATE – Quantic Foundry, via GamesMarket / Boing Boing].
- **Developers:** **52%** of GDC 2026 State of the Industry respondents see genAI as having a negative impact on the industry, up from 30% in 2025 and 18% in 2024 [ESTIMATE – GDC survey, via search summary].

**Backlash examples**
- **Clair Obscur: Expedition 33** won Indie Game of the Year and Best Debut at the Indie Game Awards on 2025-12-18. Both were **rescinded around Dec 20–22, 2025** after leftover AI placeholder textures (newspaper-style backgrounds) were found.
  - The awards went to Blue Prince (GOTY) and Sorry We're Closed (Debut). The IGA rules bar any genAI use [CONFIRMED – IGA decision, via AV Club / Malay Mail / SAYS / Gfinity].
- **Crimson Desert (Pearl Abyss, 2026):** players spotted an AI-generated battle painting with no Steam AI disclosure. The studio apologised on X and added a disclosure [CONFIRMED – Pearl Abyss statement, via TweakTown / Thred].
- **1666: Amsterdam (Panache Games):** AI-looking art in the free prologue drew backlash. All AI assets were removed before its 25 August Early Access launch. Its Steam score sits at **66% "Mixed"** [CONFIRMED – co-founder Patrice Désilets, via FinalBoss / welcome.ai].
- **Vapor World:** AI-generated cutscenes replaced its in-engine ones and drew backlash. It opened at **36% positive on 64 reviews**, and the studio pledged to remove all AI cutscenes [CONFIRMED – director statement, via Everything Edinburgh].
- **Shrine's Legacy (indie RPG):** hit by negative reviews after *false* "AI slop" accusations; the developers state it is human-made [CONFIRMED – developer statement, via Outlook Respawn]. Small teams face suspicion even without AI.
- **Storefront fatigue:** PC Gamer's "Steam Week in Review" calls spammy AI capsule art "a pox" on browsing.
  - A Game Oracle claim that "AI stigma" cuts sales by up to 50% for disclosing games could **not** be verified.
  - HTMAG asked "Did AI Slop ruin Steam Next Fest June 2026?" (2026-07-13; not read in detail).

**Risk read for a solo developer using AI:**
- Disclosure is mandatory for anything players see.
- AI-disclosed games now make up ~⅓ of releases but under-index on sales.
- Only ~8% of engaged Steam buyers reject AI outright, but ~25% of US players are less likely to buy.
- Backlash targets *visible* AI art, cutscenes and capsules most.
- **Safest approach:** use AI for code and tools (exempt from disclosure since Jan 2026), and keep visible art, key art and capsule human-made or heavily human-edited.

## Key takeaways for the risk section
1. About **20,000+ Steam releases per year**: ~19k in 2024, ~20.3k in 2025, and 20.2k already by 1 Oct 2026 (2026 is on track for a record ~24–27k).
2. **Median 2025 release grossed ~$249. ~66% grossed < $1k and ~40% < $100.** Only **~8% passed $100k** and **0.5–1.5% passed $1M** (~95–300 games).
3. Only **608 of 20,282 (2.99%)** 2025 releases reached 1,000 reviews, and about half have fewer than 10.
4. Indie ≈ **25% ($4.4–4.5B) of Steam's ~$17.7B 2025 revenue**, but the top 5 indies alone took > $500M. In 2026, new IP is only ~20% of top-500 revenue.
5. Review multipliers have fallen over time: ~93× in 2013 → ~32× in 2023 (Gamalytic) and 20–55× for 2020+ releases. Viral hits run higher (~60×), and free or cheap games run higher than expensive ones.
6. **First-week sales ≈ 0.10–0.15× launch wishlists** (median 0.15× for 25k+ wishlist games; 0.10× above $10). Year 1 ≈ 2.5× Week 1.
7. Friendslop demand is real: genre-wide concurrent players hit a record ~350k in June 2026. But each hit fades from > 50% to ~10% of genre players within 3–9 months.
8. Next Fest breakouts are rare: 3–5 games per fest reach 45k+ wishlists. Bombanana (675k fest players, 230k wishlists, 6M demo players) is an extreme outlier.
9. Mobile paid UA is getting more expensive: hybrid-casual Android CPI is up ~75% in a year to ~$0.95 globally, and US casual iOS costs $2–4. Organic short-form video and playables matter more than paid installs for a solo developer.
10. **Roblox is even more top-heavy than Steam:** the top 10 creators averaged $65.7M, while the median DevEx creator got ~$1,500 (12 months to June 2026, ~$1.7B total). Only ~100–250 accounts earned $1M+.
11. **Browser portals pay ~$0.5–4 per 1,000 plays** (derived estimate), so 1M plays ≈ $500–$4,000. Poki's top developers earn $50k–$1M a year.
12. **AI disclosure is now mainstream but under-sells:** ~31% of 2026 Steam releases disclose AI, but they take ~10–27% of sales. 8% of engaged Steam users reject AI games outright, and ~25% of US players are less likely to buy. Backlash concentrates on visible AI art.
