## Task 1 – News Briefing

**Article:** "Labor market faltered in September as jobs increased by just 29,000, unemployment rate rose to 4.2%"
**Source:** CNBC, 2 October 2026 – https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/jobs-report-september-2026.html
**Topic:** Economics / the US labour market

### Summary

According to the latest US jobs report, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on 2 October, the American labour market **lost momentum** in September. **Nonfarm payrolls** rose by only 29,000 jobs. Economists had **forecast** about 84,000, so the figure **fell well short of expectations**.

The news was even worse because of **downward revisions**. August's gain was **revised down** to 133,000, and July changed from a small gain to a **loss** of 10,000 jobs. In total, the two earlier months were 60,000 jobs weaker than first reported.

The **unemployment rate** **ticked up** to 4.2%. However, this was partly a positive sign: more people started looking for work. The **labour force participation rate** rose to a four-month high of 61.8%.

Most of the new jobs came from **health care**, **construction** and **manufacturing**. Even so, health care added only 17,000 jobs, about half its average monthly gain over the past year. **Wage growth** also slowed. **Average hourly earnings** rose 0.1% from August and 3% from a year earlier. That is the slowest annual pace since 2021.

The report matters because of **monetary policy**. In September the Federal Reserve **raised interest rates** by a quarter of a percentage point, to 3.75–4.00%. It was the first **rate hike** since 2023, and the reason was **persistent inflation**. After the weak data, investors now expect the Fed to **hold rates steady** at its October meeting. Thomas Simons, chief US economist at Jefferies, said the number should be "the nail in the coffin" for an October hike. **Stock markets rallied** on the news: the S&P 500 came close to a record high and the Nasdaq 100 reached a new peak.

**My opinion / discussion question:** The Fed is in a difficult position. Inflation is still too high, but the job market is cooling. Should the central bank keep fighting inflation, or should it protect jobs?

### Key vocabulary to activate

| Word / phrase | Meaning | Example from the topic |
|---|---|---|
| nonfarm payrolls | the number of paid jobs in the US, excluding farm work | Nonfarm payrolls rose by just 29,000. |
| to fall short of expectations | to be worse than predicted | The figure fell short of expectations. |
| forecast / consensus | what economists predicted | The consensus forecast was 84,000. |
| downward revision / to revise down | a correction that lowers earlier data | July was revised down to a loss. |
| to tick up / to edge up | to rise a little | Unemployment ticked up to 4.2%. |
| labour force participation rate | % of adults working or looking for work | Participation hit a four-month high. |
| wage growth / average hourly earnings | how fast pay is rising | Wage growth is the slowest since 2021. |
| monetary policy | central bank decisions on interest rates | The report affects monetary policy. |
| rate hike / to raise rates | an increase in interest rates | The Fed raised rates in September. |
| to hold rates steady / to stay put | to leave interest rates unchanged | Traders expect the Fed to stay put. |
| persistent inflation | inflation that stays high for a long time | The hike was driven by persistent inflation. |
| to lose momentum / to falter | to slow down or weaken | The labour market faltered. |
| to rally | (markets) to rise strongly | Stocks rallied after the report. |
| to price in | (markets) to already expect something | Investors priced in a Fed pause. |
| the nail in the coffin | the final thing that ends something | The data was the nail in the coffin for a hike. |

### Short version for presenting in class (about 1 minute)

> "I read a CNBC article about the US jobs report for September. The labour market is losing momentum: employers added only 29,000 jobs, well short of the forecast of 84,000, and earlier months were revised down by 60,000. Unemployment ticked up to 4.2%, although that's partly because more people joined the labour force. Wage growth is the slowest since 2021. This matters because the Fed raised rates in September to fight persistent inflation. Now markets expect it to hold rates steady in October, and stocks rallied on the news. My question to you: should the Fed focus on inflation or on jobs?"

## Task 2 – TED Talk: *How to Introduce Yourself — and Get Hired* (Rebecca Okamoto)

**Speaker:** Rebecca Okamoto is a communication and personal branding consultant and the founder of Evoke Strategy Group.
**Recorded:** TEDxNorthwesternU, April 2025

### Main idea

When people introduce themselves, they usually give their job title, their CV or a long, generic biography, and listeners forget it straight away. Okamoto says a good introduction should take **20 words or fewer** and should aim to make the other person say three words: **"Tell me more."** That turns an introduction into a conversation.

### The five introduction formulas

1. **Benefit introduction:** "I help *[who]* achieve *[what]*."
   *My example:* "I help small businesses understand their finances so they can grow."
2. **Breakthrough introduction:** "I help *[who]* achieve *[what]* without *[common frustration]*."
   *My example:* "I help students learn business English without memorising boring word lists."
3. **Passion introduction:** "I'm passionate about *[what]* to achieve *[what your audience values]*."
   *My example:* "I'm passionate about data analysis to help companies make smarter decisions."
4. **Strength introduction:** "I'm known for *[strength]* to achieve *[outcome]*."
   *My example:* "I'm known for staying calm under pressure, so my team delivers projects on time."
5. **Mission introduction:** "I'm on a mission to *[what you're working towards]* to achieve *[what your audience values]*."
   *My example:* "I'm on a mission to make personal finance simple for young people."

### Key takeaways

- **Focus on the listener, not on yourself.** Don't list your qualifications. Explain what you can do for *them* and what value you bring.
- **Keep it short.** Twenty words or fewer is enough to be memorable. Long bios lose attention.
- **Aim for "Tell me more."** A good introduction starts a conversation instead of ending one.
- **Use a formula.** Pick one of the five structures and adapt it to the situation: a job interview, networking or a first day at work.
- **Prepare and practise.** A strong introduction isn't improvised. Write it, test it and refine it.
- **It works for students too.** You don't need years of experience. You can talk about your strengths, passions or mission.

### My own 20-word introduction (for class)

> "I'm a business student on a mission to help companies use data to make smarter, faster decisions."
